All-Wheel Drive, and Why Tires Matter More

This is the single most expensive thing Subaru owners get wrong, and it is worth reading before you replace a tire.

Subaru's symmetrical all-wheel-drive system expects all four wheels to turn at very close to the same rate. Replacing one worn tire with a new one, or fitting a mismatched pair, leaves a difference in rolling circumference that the centre differential has to absorb continuously. On a CVT-equipped Subaru that strain goes straight into an expensive component.

  • A new tire fitted alongside three worn ones
  • Mismatched tire sizes or tread patterns between axles
  • A spare used for an extended period rather than briefly
  • Noticeable binding or shudder when turning slowly

The practical rule is to replace all four together, or to keep them within a few millimetres of tread depth. We measure and tell you honestly which applies to your car rather than selling four tires by default. Our tire and wheel alignment page covers the mounting and alignment side.

CVT Service, Done on Schedule

Most modern Subarus use a continuously variable transmission, and it is not the maintenance-free component owners are sometimes told it is. CVT fluid degrades, and the correct fluid specification is not interchangeable with conventional automatic fluid.

Shuddering when pulling away, hesitation, a whine that rises with road speed, or a transmission warning on the dash are all worth reading properly rather than guessing at. We check fluid condition, read transmission fault codes rather than powertrain codes alone, and road test to reproduce what you are describing.

Boxer Engines and What They Ask For

Subaru's flat-four layout is what gives these cars their low centre of gravity, and it also gives them a characteristic set of things to watch.

  • External oil leaks, which these engines are prone to showing
  • Head gasket concerns on older boxer engines
  • Oil consumption between services
  • Cooling system, thermostats and water pumps
  • Wheel bearings, which work hard on an all-wheel-drive car
  • Suspension bushings and struts

Head gasket concerns on older engines in particular deserve a real diagnosis rather than a verdict. There is a large difference between a genuine head gasket failure and an external oil leak that looks alarming from underneath, and the two carry very different bills. We test rather than assume. See engine repair for how we approach diagnosis generally.

Subarus and Scarborough Winters

These cars are bought here for exactly the weather that is hardest on them. All-wheel drive means Subarus get driven through conditions that keep other cars parked, and the underside pays for it.

Road salt attacks brake lines, exhaust components, subframes and suspension mounts, and rear wheel bearings work harder on an all-wheel-drive car than most owners expect. Winter driving also means more short trips with the engine never fully warming, which is hard on oil. If your Subaru is doing genuine winter duty, the underbody deserves a look each spring.

Subaru Body and Collision Repair

Body and collision work is done here rather than sublet, which matters more on an all-wheel-drive car than it might sound. A knock heavy enough to move a panel can also affect suspension geometry and the sensors sitting behind the bumper covers, and on a Subaru anything that alters ride height or wheel alignment feeds straight back into the driveline.

Everyday damage is the bulk of it: parking-lot door dents, bumper scuffs, and rust starting at the wheel arches and rocker panels after a few salted winters. Paint is colour-matched, and because the mechanical side is in the same building we can check that nothing underneath moved at the same time. There is more on auto body and collision repair generally.

Subaru FAQ

Common Subaru Questions

Do I really have to replace all four tires on a Subaru?

Usually yes, or keep all four within a few millimetres of each other. Symmetrical all-wheel drive expects the wheels to turn at very close to the same rate, and a mismatch leaves the centre differential absorbing that difference continuously. On a CVT-equipped car that strain reaches an expensive component. We measure your tread depths and tell you honestly whether one, two or four is the right answer.

Does a Subaru CVT need servicing?

Yes. CVT fluid degrades and the specification is not interchangeable with conventional automatic transmission fluid. Shuddering when pulling away, hesitation, or a whine that rises with speed are worth reading properly. We check fluid condition and read transmission fault codes rather than powertrain codes alone before recommending anything.

My Subaru is leaking oil. Is it the head gasket?

Not necessarily, and it is worth finding out before committing to the work. These engines are prone to external oil leaks that look alarming from underneath but are far cheaper to address than a head gasket. The two carry very different bills, so we test rather than assume.

Do you work on Subaru as well as European cars?

Yes. Subaru is Japanese rather than European, and we service import and domestic vehicles generally alongside the European makes we hold factory software for. Call (416) 913-2394 with your model and year.
