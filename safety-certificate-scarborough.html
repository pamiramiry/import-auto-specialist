We Issue the Certificate Here

Import Auto Specialist is a licensed Motor Vehicle Inspection Station, and the inspection is carried out by a licensed 310S technician. That means the whole thing happens in one place: we inspect the vehicle, carry out any repairs it needs to meet the standard, and issue the Safety Standards Certificate on site once it passes. You are not sent somewhere else to get the paperwork done.

An SSC is the document the Ministry of Transportation requires to plate a vehicle in a new owner's name, and it can only be issued by a licensed station. If a seller offers you a "safety" from somewhere that is not licensed, it is not a valid certificate.

When You Need a Safety Certificate

Most people book because a transaction requires it rather than because something is wrong with the car. The common reasons:

  • Selling a vehicle privately — the buyer needs a valid certificate to plate it
  • Buying privately, where the car is being sold "as-is" without one
  • Registering a vehicle brought in from another province
  • Putting a vehicle back on the road after it has been unplated or stored
  • Rebuilt vehicles being returned to the road

Timing matters more than people expect. In Ontario a Safety Standards Certificate is valid for 36 days from the date of inspection, so book close to the point of sale or registration rather than weeks ahead. Call (416) 913-2394 and we will help you time it.

What the Inspection Covers

A safety inspection is a defined, standardised check against provincial requirements, not a general opinion of the car. The vehicle goes up on the hoist with the wheels off and we work through the required systems: brakes and brake lines, steering and suspension components, tires and wheels, lights and electrical, the exhaust system, glass, wipers and mirrors, seatbelts, and the structure and underbody.

Each item either meets the standard or it does not, and there is no discretion in that. What we can do is tell you clearly which items failed, why, and what it takes to bring them up to standard, so you are working from a real list rather than a bare pass-or-fail verdict.

What Happens If Something Fails

If an item does not meet the standard, we go through the list with you and give you an estimate before any repair work begins. You are not obliged to have that work done here — you can take the list elsewhere, and the inspection still stands.

That is worth stating plainly, because it is what people are most wary about when the same shop both inspects and repairs. The standard decides what fails; we do not. We would rather hand you an honest list and lose the repair than pad it.

What a Certificate Does and Does Not Tell You

A Safety Standards Certificate records that the vehicle met the minimum provincial safety standard on the day it was inspected. It is a point-in-time legal document, not a statement about the car's overall condition or how long it will last.

This matters most if you are buying. A car can hold a valid certificate and still be close to needing a timing chain, a transmission service, or major rust work, because none of those are safety items. If what you want to know is whether the car is a good buy rather than whether it can be plated, a pre-purchase inspection is the check that answers it. The two are different, and buyers often want both.

Why Scarborough Cars Fail

The items that most often fail here are predictable and almost all corrosion-related. Road salt attacks brake lines, exhaust systems, suspension components, and underbody structure, and a car that spends its winters in the GTA ages faster underneath than its mileage suggests.

Brake lines are the one worth knowing about, because they corrode from the outside in and give very little warning before they fail — and they are a straightforward fail on inspection. Potholed spring roads add worn tie rod ends, ball joints, and bent wheels to the list. None of this is unusual for a local vehicle; it just means an older car is worth inspecting before you are standing in front of a buyer with a deposit.

Safety Certificate FAQ

Common Safety Certificate Questions

How long is a Safety Standards Certificate valid?

In Ontario a Safety Standards Certificate is valid for 36 days from the date of inspection. Because that window is short, it is best to book close to the point of sale or registration rather than weeks in advance. Call (416) 913-2394 and we will help you time it.

Can you issue the certificate, or do I go somewhere else?

We issue it here. Import Auto Specialist is a licensed Motor Vehicle Inspection Station and the inspection is done by a licensed 310S technician, so the inspection, any repairs needed to meet the standard, and the certificate itself all happen in one place.

What happens if my vehicle fails?

We go through the failed items with you, explain why each one did not meet the standard, and give you a clear estimate before any repair work starts. You are under no obligation to have the repairs done here — you can take the list elsewhere.

Is a safety certificate the same as a pre-purchase inspection?

No, and they answer different questions. A safety certificate records that the vehicle met the minimum provincial safety standard on the day it was inspected, and it is what you need to plate the car. A pre-purchase inspection is a broader assessment of overall condition and what the vehicle is likely to need, which is what you want when deciding whether to buy it at all.
