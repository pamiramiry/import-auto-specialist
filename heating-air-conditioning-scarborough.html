Signs Your Heating or A/C Needs Work

Climate-control faults tend to arrive gradually, so it is easy to adapt to them until the weather makes them impossible to ignore. Any of these is worth checking:

  • A/C that blows warm, or cools far less than it used to
  • No heat, or heat that only arrives after a long drive
  • A musty or sour smell when the fan first comes on
  • A blower that works only on certain fan speeds
  • Windows that fog up and will not clear
  • Rattling, squealing, or clicking when the A/C engages
  • A sweet smell or damp carpet in the front footwells

A sweet smell with damp carpet usually means a leaking heater core, and that one is worth looking at promptly because the coolant it is losing belongs in the engine.

Comfortable in Every Season

Whether it's an air conditioner that's lost its cool before summer or a heater that won't warm up in a Scarborough winter, we diagnose and repair the whole climate-control system: refrigerant, compressor, blower motor, heater core, controls, and the cabin air filter. We find why it's not working before recommending a fix, then give you an estimate before any work.

Typical issues we see are weak or no cooling, no heat, a musty smell when the fan comes on (often a clogged cabin filter or a dirty evaporator), a blower that only works on some speeds, and foggy windows that won't clear. We use parts and refrigerant matched to your vehicle so it performs the way it should. Refrigerant work is handled by an HRAI-certified technician, so the system is charged and sealed to spec.

Why a Top-Up Is Not a Repair

Air conditioning is a sealed system. If it is low on refrigerant, that refrigerant went somewhere, and adding more without finding the leak simply buys a few weeks before you are back where you started. It also lets the system run low on the oil that circulates with the refrigerant, and a compressor starved of oil is a much larger bill than the seal or hose that was leaking.

We test the system and locate the leak first. Once we know it holds, we evacuate and recharge it to the exact weight your vehicle calls for, because both under- and over-charging make an A/C system cool poorly.

Heat and Defrost Are a Safety System Here

In a Scarborough winter, climate control stops being about comfort. Defrost is what clears an iced windscreen and keeps it clear, and a car whose heater cannot keep up is a car you cannot see out of properly on a February morning. Ontario's rules on driving with obstructed vision are not lenient, and a failed blower or a plugged cabin filter is enough to cause it.

The other half of the year brings the opposite problem: humid GTA summers put the A/C under sustained load in stop-and-go traffic, which is exactly the condition that exposes a marginal compressor or a partially blocked condenser. Both systems are cheapest to fix in the shoulder season, before you actually need them.

Cabin Air Quality and That Musty Smell

The smell that appears for the first few seconds after you switch the fan on is one of the most common complaints we hear, and it is rarely the refrigerant. Moisture condenses on the evaporator every time you run the A/C, and if it does not drain and dry properly it grows bacteria and mould in a dark box behind your dashboard. A cabin air filter left in place too long makes it worse and restricts airflow at the same time.

We check the cabin filter, the evaporator drain, and the evaporator itself rather than masking the smell with a spray. On many import vehicles the cabin filter is genuinely awkward to reach, which is precisely why it so often turns out never to have been changed.

Heating & A/C FAQ

Common Heating & A/C Questions

Why is my A/C blowing warm?

It could be low refrigerant from a leak, a failing compressor, or an electrical fault. We test the system to find the actual cause rather than just topping it up, so the fix lasts.

Do you do A/C recharges?

We diagnose first. A recharge without finding the leak is only temporary. Once we know the system holds, we can recharge it to spec.

Why does my A/C smell musty when I turn it on?

Almost always moisture sitting on the evaporator behind the dash, often combined with a cabin air filter that is overdue. We check the filter, the evaporator drain, and the evaporator itself rather than covering the smell with a deodorising spray, because the spray wears off and the cause does not.

Should I get the A/C looked at before summer?

It is a good idea. A system that has quietly lost refrigerant over the winter will still feel acceptable on a mild spring day and then fall short in July.
